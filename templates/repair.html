Repair Before Replace

A serviceable roof deserves the opportunity to keep performing.

A roof is one of the largest investments a property owner will make. When that roof is still within its serviceable life, an isolated leak or damaged detail does not necessarily justify replacing the entire system.

Storm damage, punctures, flashing failures, loose seams, aging sealants, and drainage issues can all create problems long before a roof has reached the end of its useful life. Timely, properly planned repairs can correct those conditions while preserving the investment already made in the roof.

Our goal is not simply to stop today’s leak. It is to help you receive the full practical service life from the roofing system you already own.

Looking Beyond the Leak

The location of an interior leak is not always where water entered the roof.

Water can travel through insulation, follow the flutes of a metal roof deck, move along structural components, or collect within an existing roofing assembly before it becomes visible inside the building.

What appears to be one leak may also have more than one entry point. An open flashing lap, a loose membrane seam, and a deteriorated roof penetration can all contribute water to the same interior location.

That is why our evaluation does not stop after we find the first possible problem. We inspect the surrounding area and look for every condition that may be contributing to the leak.

Evaluating the Entire Roof System

Some problems are isolated. Others are signs of a developing condition.

A puncture caused by storm debris, a dropped tool, or work by another trade may require one properly completed repair. Other deficiencies require a broader evaluation.

Loose wall flashing, open field laps, deteriorated sealants, and repeated failures within the same area can indicate that nearby materials are approaching a similar condition. Those components were often installed at the same time and have experienced the same weather, temperature changes, expansion, contraction, and building movement.

For example, finding two feet of open flashing does not necessarily mean that repairing only those two feet provides the best long-term result. Inspecting and repairing a broader section may address adjacent material before it becomes the source of another leak.

When the crew is already mobilized and the necessary repair materials are already on site, addressing the surrounding area can also make better use of the customer’s investment and reduce the likelihood of another service visit in the near future.

Emergency Roof Service

Protecting the building may require an immediate temporary repair.

Active leaks do not always occur under conditions that allow a permanent repair to be completed immediately. Severe weather, wet roofing materials, unsafe working conditions, or the need to obtain system-specific materials can affect what work can be performed during the initial service visit.

In those situations, a temporary repair may be the responsible first step. Its purpose is to limit further water entry and help protect the building until conditions allow the permanent work to be completed correctly.

We explain what was done, whether additional work is needed, and what the next step should be.

Preventative Maintenance

Small roofing issues are easier to manage before they become active leaks.

Most roofing systems do not fail all at once. Problems often develop gradually as sealants age, flashings loosen, seams open, drains collect debris, and minor damage remains unnoticed.

Semi-annual or annual roof inspections can identify those conditions while they are still manageable. Keeping the roof clean, maintaining drainage, repairing deficiencies promptly, and completing preventative work later in the membrane’s life can significantly extend its serviceable life.

Proper maintenance cannot prevent building settlement, eliminate every drainage limitation, or stop normal aging. It can, however, help a roofing system perform well beyond the length of its original manufacturer’s warranty when its condition and construction allow it.

A maintenance visit may include:

  • General roof-condition inspection
  • Removal of loose debris
  • Drain, gutter, and scupper review
  • Inspection of seams and flashings
  • Review of roof penetrations and equipment curbs
  • Identification of punctures or membrane damage
  • Documentation of developing conditions
  • Recommendations for needed repairs
